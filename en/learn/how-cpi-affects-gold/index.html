Gold trading Q&A

How does CPI affect the gold price?

Short answer

US CPI is released by the Bureau of Labor Statistics at 8:30 New York time. A hotter-than-expected reading usually lifts US yields and the dollar and cuts the odds of Fed rate cuts, which tends to push gold down; a cooler reading tends to lift gold. What matters is the surprise versus the forecast, especially in core CPI.

What to watch

  • Headline CPI and core CPI (excluding food and energy), month-on-month and year-on-year.
  • The forecast (consensus) — the market reacts to the difference, not the number itself.
  • Revisions and the details (shelter, services) that traders discuss in the first minutes.

Typical reaction pattern

The first one to five minutes after 8:30 ET are often a spike in both directions as spreads widen. The more reliable move usually appears after the first 15-minute candle closes, once yields and the dollar have settled. A reading in line with forecasts can still move gold if expectations had shifted beforehand.

Practical rules

  • Do not hold a full-size position into the release unless the stop already accounts for a 20–40 dollar spike.
  • Mark the pre-release high and low; a 15-minute close outside them is more meaningful than the first wick.
  • Compare with the US 10-year yield: if gold and yields move the same direction, the reaction is less trustworthy.

Quick answers

When is CPI released?

Monthly, at 8:30 a.m. New York time, usually around the middle of the month. Check the official BLS schedule.

Is CPI more important than NFP for gold?

Both are major. When inflation is the Fed's main concern, CPI often moves gold more.
