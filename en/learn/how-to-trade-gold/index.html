Gold trading Q&A

How to trade gold (XAUUSD) step by step

Short answer

Start from the daily chart to know the bigger direction, mark the few price zones where buyers or sellers stepped in before, decide in advance which level would prove your idea wrong, size the position so that level costs no more than 1% of your account, and only act when price reaches your zone — not in the middle of nowhere.

1. Know the bigger picture first

Look at the daily (D1) and 4-hour (H4) charts before anything else. Are highs and lows getting higher, lower, or going sideways? Where did yesterday close compared with the day before? Trading against a strong daily move is where most gold losses come from.

2. Mark only the zones that matter

Pick two to four zones above and below price: previous day high and low, the daily open, areas where price turned sharply before. A zone is a band (for example 4048–4056), not a single line — gold regularly overshoots a line by a few dollars.

3. Write down what would make you wrong

Before entering, name the level where the idea is invalid — for example "a 15-minute close above 4083". If you cannot name it, you do not have a trade, you have a hope.

4. Size the position from that level

Risk a fixed small share of the account per idea; many traders use 0.5–1%. If the stop is 8 dollars away and you risk 100 dollars, the size is 100 / (8 × 100) = 0.125 lots on a 100-ounce contract.

5. Respect the calendar and the session

Check for US data (CPI, jobs report, Fed decisions) before trading. Gold is quietest in the Asia session and most active when London and New York overlap. Many traders simply stand aside in the minutes around red-flag news.

Quick answers

How much money do I need to trade gold?

With micro lots (0.01 = about 1 dollar per 1-dollar move on a 100-ounce contract) small accounts can trade gold, but the account must be big enough that a normal stop is no more than about 1% of it.

Is gold good for beginners?

Gold is liquid and well covered, but it moves fast. Beginners should trade small size, avoid major news releases and follow a written plan.
