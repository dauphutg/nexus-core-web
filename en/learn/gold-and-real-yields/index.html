Gold trading Q&A

What is the relationship between gold and real yields?

Short answer

Real yields are bond yields after inflation, usually measured by the 10-year US TIPS yield. Gold pays no interest, so when real yields rise, holding gold costs more in lost income and gold tends to fall; when real yields fall, gold tends to rise. The link was very strong for years but has weakened since 2022 as central banks bought record amounts of gold.

Why it matters

Large investors compare gold with what they could earn in safe bonds after inflation. A rise in real yields from, say, 1% to 2% makes the bond alternative noticeably more attractive.

How to watch it

  • US 10-year TIPS yield (real yield).
  • US 10-year nominal yield as a quick intraday proxy.
  • The US 2-year yield for short-term Fed expectations.

The recent exception

In 2022⁠–⁠2024 real yields rose sharply, yet gold went on to record highs. Record central-bank purchases, more than 1,000 tonnes a year according to the World Gold Council, and geopolitical demand offset the yield effect. Yields still move gold on data days, but they are not the only driver.

Quick answers

Where can I see real yields?

The 10-year TIPS yield is published by the US Treasury and widely shown on charting platforms.

Do rising nominal yields always hurt gold?

Not if inflation expectations rise just as fast, then real yields stay flat.
