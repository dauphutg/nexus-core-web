How to read a daily gold trading plan

Short answer

A good gold plan tells you five things: the bias (which side has the edge today), the main zone where it expects a reaction, the targets, the exact level that makes the view wrong, and the news that could change everything. Read the invalidation first — it tells you how much the plan can cost if it fails.

The parts of a plan

  • Bias — SELL, BUY, two-way or stand aside, with a lean when the plan is two-way.
  • Main zone — the band where the plan expects price to react.
  • Targets — where the move is expected to run if the zone holds (T1 first).
  • Invalidation — the level or candle close that cancels the idea.
  • Scenarios with rough probabilities, and the economic calendar.

How to use it

Do not chase price toward the zone. Wait for price to reach it, watch the reaction on a lower timeframe, and size your position from the invalidation level. If price never reaches the zone, the plan simply did not trigger — that is not a loss.

A plan written before the session is a map, not a promise. When the invalidation hits, the plan is wrong and should be dropped, not defended.

What does a two-way bias mean?

The plan sees a range: it would sell near resistance and buy near support, sometimes leaning to one side.
