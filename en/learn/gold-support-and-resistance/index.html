How to find key support and resistance levels for gold today

Short answer

The levels that matter most for gold on any given day are usually the previous day's high and low, today's daily open, round numbers, and recent zones where price reversed sharply on the 1-hour or 4-hour chart. Treat each as a zone a few dollars wide, and rank them by how many times price reacted there.

Where to look

  • Previous day high and low (PDH / PDL) — the first places stops cluster.
  • Today's daily open — price often returns to test it.
  • Round numbers such as 4000 or 4050 — orders gather there.
  • H4 and H1 swing highs and lows where price turned with a strong candle.
  • Gaps or fast moves that left little trading behind (price likes to revisit them).

Zones, not lines

Gold routinely pokes through a level by a few dollars before turning. Drawing a band — say 4048.4 to 4056.2 — and waiting for a reaction inside it avoids being stopped out by noise. The wider the recent daily range, the wider the zone should be.

Support turns into resistance

When a support zone breaks and price closes clearly below it, that same zone often acts as resistance on the way back up — and the other way round. Many of the best gold setups are retests of a freshly broken zone.

Related questions

How to use the daily open line when trading gold

Quick answers

Which timeframe is best for gold support and resistance?

Use D1 and H4 to find the zones and H1 or M15 to watch the reaction. Levels from higher timeframes carry more weight.

How many levels should I draw?

Two to four on each side is enough. A chart full of lines makes every price look like a level.
