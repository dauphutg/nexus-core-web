How to judge a gold analysis or signal service: the track record test

Short answer

Trust a gold analysis service only if it publishes every call before the move, keeps the losing ones on the record, defines in advance what counts as right or wrong, and reports results in R (risk units) rather than "pips won". If any of those is missing, the record cannot be checked.

Five questions to ask

  1. Is every plan or signal time-stamped before the move?
  2. Are losses and missed calls still visible?
  3. Are the grading rules written down and applied the same way every day?
  4. Is performance shown in R or percent of risk, not only in pips?
  5. Is there enough history, dozens of calls across different market conditions?

Red flags

  • Screenshots of winning trades only.
  • Results that "start" after a good month.
  • Calls edited after the fact, or targets without a stop.
  • Guaranteed returns or pressure to join quickly.

Quick answers

What is a good win rate?

It depends on the reward-to-risk ratio; a 40% win rate can be profitable at 2R per win. Total R tells you more than win rate.

Is past performance a guarantee?

No. A track record shows how a method behaved; it cannot promise future results.
