Gold trading Q&A

What is a liquidity sweep in XAUUSD?

Short answer

A liquidity sweep is a quick move just beyond an obvious high or low — where many stop orders sit — followed by a fast return inside the range. In gold it often happens at the previous day high or low or at round numbers. The sweep fills large orders using those stops; the reversal after it can be a strong signal.

How it looks on the chart

  • Price pushes a few dollars past a clear high or low.
  • The candle closes back inside the range, often with a long wick.
  • The next candles move away from the swept level with momentum.

Sweep or breakout?

The difference is acceptance. A breakout holds beyond the level and closes there on the 15-minute or 1-hour chart; a sweep cannot stay there. Wait for the candle close instead of reacting to the first wick.

Where sweeps are most common

At the London open and around US data, when volume arrives suddenly. Asia highs and lows are frequent targets during London.

Risk

Not every wick is a sweep. Place the invalidation beyond the sweep's extreme, and size accordingly — sweeps can extend further than expected on news.

Quick answers

Is a stop hunt the same thing?

In everyday use, yes — the term describes price running the stops before reversing.

Which timeframe shows sweeps best?

M15 and H1 for intraday; D1 for sweeps of the previous day's high or low.
